## Objective and evidence boundary

The measured objective is at most 30 seconds from process start to healthy attached chat on routine clean startup with a newly detected save published, journaled and settled, and on an acknowledged-binding restart; healthy recovery remains at most 180 seconds. For a counted healthy timing result, an enabled watcher must have a running startup observation, alongside fresh admitted state, runtime consumer ownership, healthy journal, ready safety, a bound ready provider and verified startup-worker stop at attachment. Watcher status remains visible, but a disabled or stopped watcher does not itself prevent chat attachment or provider input under the runtime correctness contract; acquisition still checks state directly. Such a start is reported separately from the healthy timing target when the watcher was enabled but stopped. A phase-D-through-H fault may still attach a status-only client when provider safety refuses, or a degraded ready client when safety allows the final provider start, but its elapsed time and fault are reported separately and do not satisfy either healthy target. A start whose bound provider is ready but whose status is not healthy attaches that provider with its degraded status, and is likewise reported separately. Interactive credential login is excluded. The fixed current-source 120-second interaction gate is measured and tested separately, without changing its deadline, acceptance or result semantics. Presentation changes remain deferred.

The [copied-save timing controls](../reports/runtime-startup-copied-save-controls-2026-10-14.md) record 72.568 seconds from immediately before measured `openBackend` to in-memory fake-client attachment on the current path, and 48.338 seconds over the same broad boundary in a separate optimized scratch source snapshot with 2.406 seconds of explicit test-only job rechecks. A separate stub scheduling pair reached a 38.089-second synthetic fake-provider and verified-stop boundary without client attachment. Fixture staging and process launch are outside these clocks; none uses the real provider or client. These are distinct feasibility observations, not additive savings or proof of healthy 30. Earlier source-checked startup controls and their limits are recorded in [runtime startup spikes](../reports/runtime-startup-spikes-2026-10-14.md) and [runtime core startup feasibility](../reports/runtime-core-startup-feasibility-2026-10-14.md). A target miss never removes an admission, warning, write postcondition or worker-stop proof.

Later copied-root, process-to-attachment controls on the private fast path used a six-second owned synthetic provider. Two reversed-order pairs held guarded batching, current-source safety and final proof fixed while moving only synthetic provider start: late starts took 36.682 and 35.519 seconds, and overlapped starts took 30.283 and 29.527 seconds; one overlapped run missed 30. Three acknowledged synthetic restarts took 22.696, 22.245 and 22.277 seconds, and one narrow interrupted-marker-residue recovery took 29.120 seconds. These are copied saves and fake provider/client observations, not real SDK readiness or general recovery proof. Their result files are not cited and could not be located on 2026-10-19, so they are unverified context; later fixture timings with retained results are in [selected-image fixture evidence](../reports/runtime-selected-image-fixture-evidence-20261019.md). A separate compiled source-hash spike observed about 0.6 seconds in two matched copied pairs and was excluded from the earlier worker candidate; source-bound image admission is governed separately below. The default-profile SDK write footprint, pre-guard whole-process write proof and repeated real-provider 30/180-second results remain open.

## Exact authority changes

[Go native helper](runtime-powershell-dependency.md) replaces the `startupMetadata` and `startupMutation` workers of this section and the one-shot job observations routed around them with its helper service; `startupCoreReply` keeps its role. Chat startup has two source-attested worker opening boundaries. In the explicitly selected fast path, after the held instance guard, the host positively proves the named `startupMetadata` predecessor job absent through a fresh one-shot job observation, made by the compiled native observer when the image pins are ready, obtains complete source evidence, opens one read-only metadata worker and validates its exact scope, source and nonce hello. With the scope-source batch, that absence proof precedes immutable scope binding and one observation returns the existing binding's bytes with the source evidence; without the batch, the binding precedes the absence proof. Only then may it route fresh path admission, hash, Inventory/Marker and unowned job observations through that worker during guarded phase-C residue cleanup, runtimeMarker reservation, prior-marker read, watcher-orphan reconciliation and all-role admission. It cannot mutate, consume, publish the marker or prove its own job membership. Residue cleanup still proves `publication` and `startupMutation` jobs absent before scanning and each deletion; before marker publication, the host rechecks the worker source and scope, reconciles any watcher, and admits every process role, allowing only that exact owned metadata job and its verified system console host. A failed prior marker, watcher check, source proof or predecessor admission stops before a new marker write, then closes the early worker and verifies its job absent before releasing the guard. Admitted residue already removed is not rolled back. A failed or uncertain worker stop retains the guard while this process lives. With the fast path disabled, `startupMetadata` keeps the agreed post-admission opening seam; an absent ordinary `.tmp` keeps the one-shot path. `startupCoreReply` and `startupMutation` always open after marker reservation, prior-marker read, watcher reconciliation and complete predecessor admission. Their opening and the existing one-shot marker publication may proceed concurrently, but they route no request until the marker's exact postcondition and their own source-attested hellos pass. If marker publication or any hello fails, await the in-flight marker operation's reconciled outcome, stop launched workers, prove their jobs absent and follow the existing failure or verified-absence fallback. No worker grants scope, guard, marker, watcher or predecessor authority.

`startupMetadata` keeps read-only admission, hash, Inventory/Marker, event inspection, source evidence and fresh scoped job-observation modes. With the fast selector enabled, it opens after the guarded one-shot absence and complete source proof described above, and may answer those read-only requests before marker publication. The host does not use it to prove its own job membership, and job observations for the `metadata`, `startupMetadata` and `startupCoreReply` roles always use fresh one-shot observations, because their stop proofs can be awaited from inside the inspector queue that this worker serves. Once the worker has stopped verifiably, the host detaches it and every later job observation uses a fresh one-shot observation. When the compiled image pins are ready, every unowned job observation by the guarded backend's supervisor instead uses the compiled native one-shot observer, while the pre-guard read-only inspection still proves its own metadata job absent through a one-shot PowerShell observation when it closes, so among selected chats the warm worker answers job observations only on the absent-image path; the private `fastCopiedStartup` option, which selects no images, also routes them to it unless it supplies its own private native job observer. Before each request, the host checks the held guard, exact scope and source evidence; after each response it retains the existing result validation and use-time recheck. If early opening is unavailable with verified job absence, the host may use fresh one-shot observations or a later post-marker worker; source, scope or stop uncertainty fails closed. With the selector disabled, it opens after watcher reconciliation and all-role admission and routes no request until marker completion. An absent ordinary companion `.tmp` chooses the one-shot route for the whole startup, while invalid, unreadable or redirected `.tmp` fails closed. No worker creates `.tmp` or uses a compiler directory under runtime-data. Workers use ordinary companion `.tmp` as PowerShell compiler scratch, except on the selected path, where the helper scratch environment's own temporary folder under the installation's `.tmp` replaces it.

`startupCoreReply` is a new read-only process role. It warms exactly the current `Read-CoreReply.ps1` transformation for `Publish`, `Settlement` and `Consumer`, using the same admitted `core/DigestDiff.psm1`, its imported private scripts, and runtime native sources. It receives frozen captured core exit code, stdout and stderr after the original `Publish-StateEvent.ps1`, `Set-StateEventSettlement.ps1` or `Set-CompanionConsumer.ps1` child has terminated and its `core` job is verified absent. It returns the existing version-1 core-reply document, which `CoreClient.validate` and its current operation-specific TypeScript checks still validate; a worker success never turns an invalid core result into success. The worker does no core command, consume, settlement, cache, state write or result caching, and it closes request-local source handles before replying. The original core scripts, `CoreClient` queue, checked safety intent, journal append between Publish and Settlement, core-command process-stop proof, failure classifications and deadline accounting remain unchanged. No retained core-command host is introduced.

[Go native helper](runtime-powershell-dependency.md) replaces the `startupMutation` worker with its helper service and moves the pre-marker and final clean-shutdown marker writes from the one-shot `publication` role to that service, under a narrowed `cleanShutdown: true` that attests every role except the helper service. `startupMutation` is the sole warm mutating role. Under the host's existing `FilePublisher.exclusive` queue it executes the native work currently reached through `Write-RuntimeFile.ps1`, ordinary `Invoke-PayloadCodec.ps1` and the full `ResourceInventory.TrimQuarantine` operation. The host still owns guard, scope, path, capacity, reservation, prospective-budget, staged-input and protected-name checks; the native routine independently validates its inputs. The queue serializes publication, codec and Quarantine trim, including error and cleanup paths. The worker accepts one request at a time, performs no independent reservation or second publication attempt, retains no target handle or previous payload, and closes request-local handles before replying. The host keeps frozen input/control hashes, exact result parsing, destination readback, cleanup of only owned stages, deadline checks and invalidation. Its PowerShell compiler scratch follows the worker rule above, never runtime-data `.tmp`; targets remain inside the admitted scope. The one-shot `publication` role remains for pre-marker and final clean-shutdown marker writes and verified-absence fallback. Ordinary non-chat commands and later turns retain their one-shot routes.

The three worker roles do not relax `ResourceAuthority`, `ScopeStorage`, `OperationStore`, journal, safety, recovery or provider admission. A worker is an execution transport, not an authority cache. Every request retains fresh caller observation, binding and reservation, and every reply retains host validation and use-time recheck. A typed native refusal never causes a permissive retry. A launch failure with positively verified job absence, or an absent ordinary `.tmp`, permits a fresh one-shot route for an independent operation. Source admission, hello or drift mismatch, malformed or unsolicited output, request timeout, uncertain mutation outcome and uncertain stop fail the dependent operation closed. A failed or absent worker supplies no stale reply.

## Batched source proof and wire contracts

[Go native helper](runtime-powershell-dependency.md) retires this evidence call with the metadata and mutation bundles; the core-reply digest comes from the helper service's `coreReplySource` request. `Get-StartupWorkerSource.ps1` is a supervised one-shot read-only helper with exact `-CompanionRoot <held companion root> -DataRoot <held scope root> -SourceRoot <admitted runtime installation root>` and optional `-IncludeScopeBytes`, and no stdin. All paths are nonempty normalized absolute paths; DataRoot is the bound child of CompanionRoot, while SourceRoot may be a separate admitted installation in a private fixture. Its stdout is one strict JSON object under the existing 65,536-byte metadata capture bound, with empty stderr on success. A nonzero exit, malformed, partial, extra-field or oversized result is source-evidence failure, never evidence of absence. It emits no path or source bytes except the optional bounded `scopeBytesBase64` field described below and has no mutation authority.

One supervised read-only evidence call checks ordinary physically admitted companion `.tmp`, exact scope binding bytes and three independently named ordered source bundles. It admits each source under its owning root, requires an ordinary singly linked file, reads at most 1,048,576 bytes, re-admits and re-reads identical bytes and identity, then hashes ordered four-byte unsigned big-endian length plus exact bytes per member with SHA-256. Missing, unreadable, changed, extra or aliased source fails the call. An absent ordinary `.tmp` returns exactly `{status:"absent"}` and creates nothing. With `-IncludeScopeBytes`, a fresh native admission that positively proves `scope.json` absent returns exactly `{status:"scopeAbsent"}`; the host then performs guarded `initializeScope` and takes new complete source evidence before any worker opening. A helper error or uncertain absence never selects that branch. A present result without the switch has exactly `{status:"present",scopeSha256,metadataSha256,coreReplySha256,mutationSha256}`; with the switch it also has `scopeBytesBase64`, the canonical base64 of the exact scope bytes when at most 32,768 bytes and null otherwise. Digests are 64 lowercase hex; the scope digest must equal the held binding, and a null optional byte field requires the ordinary admitted binding read. No partial bundle authorizes a worker. Evidence lives in startup memory only, and each worker independently re-admits and hashes its own source for hello and before every request. A changed source rejects the next use; a completed request under pinned code is not retroactively reinterpreted. The next startup takes new evidence.

[Go native helper](runtime-powershell-dependency.md) replaces the `startupMetadata` and `startupMutation` grammars in this and the following paragraphs with its helper service protocol; the `startupCoreReply` grammar stays. All three workers use a four-byte unsigned big-endian length and strict UTF-8 JSON, with input length 1 through 2,097,152 bytes, output length 1 through 65,536 bytes except that `startupMetadata` permits up to 131,072 bytes for `eventBatch`, no duplicate or unknown fields, unsolicited stderr, extra frame or trailing bytes. Its other results remain within 65,536 bytes. The first output is exactly `{version:1,nonce,scopeSha256,sourceSha256}`: `nonce` is a fresh 32-lowercase-hex spawn challenge, and scope and source hashes are the held 64-lowercase-hex digests. The bounded handshake deadline is at most 30,000 milliseconds from spawn including child identity and hello, without restart at child-ready. The core command's original deadline is not renewed for the validator.

For `startupMutation`, `op` is `publish`, `codec` or `quarantineTrim`, and `root` is the held data root. `publish` and `codec` add exact `target`, `inputFile`, `inputSha256`; the paths are normalized absolute paths inside that root and the hash is 64-lowercase-hex. `publish` adds exact `profile` (`config` or `state-event`) and Boolean `createOnly`, with no codec fields; its result is the existing exact `{published,sha256,cleanupComplete,retainedSiblings}` object. `codec` adds exact `controlFile`, `controlSha256`, Boolean `verifyOnly` and Boolean `preview`, with no publication fields; its result is the existing exact `{codecVersion,sha256,published,cleanupComplete,metadata}` object. ## Failure and durable-state lifecycle

[Go native helper](runtime-powershell-dependency.md) narrows this lifecycle to the core-reply worker and moves the pre-marker and final clean-shutdown marker writes from the one-shot publication path to its helper service, under a narrowed `cleanShutdown: true` that attests every role except the helper service; its helper service lives for the whole runtime process under that design's own lifecycle. Before healthy, degraded or status-only client attachment, not-owner return, normal shutdown or guard release, stop new requests, settle or reject queued work, close each worker and positively verify every startup-worker job absent. The final clean-shutdown marker stays on the one-shot publication path after those roles and any provider candidate are quiescent. A worker close failure retains the guard while the process lives and prevents clean marker or attachment. A crash leaves the next start's full role admission responsible for any survivor; inability to prove absence blocks startup. Marker, outbox, journal, safety and provider state keep their existing durable writers and readers.

Before marker publication, an absent ordinary `.tmp` selects one-shot transports without creating it; invalid, unreadable or redirected `.tmp`, failed source evidence, prior-watcher uncertainty or incomplete predecessor admission stops with the previous marker unchanged. In the fast path the early metadata worker may already have spawned or served read-only requests during guarded residue cleanup. Before each selected sibling deletion, writer-job absence and exact target admission still hold. If a prior marker, watcher, source, scope or predecessor check then fails, close the early worker, prove its job absent and retain the previous marker; do not undo an admitted deletion. If worker stop is uncertain, keep the guard while this process lives. A crash before new marker publication leaves the previous marker and remaining residue; the next guarded start must admit all predecessors, including any surviving early worker.

Once marker publication starts concurrently with post-admission worker opening and hello, the target may remain old, be staged with tmp or bak residue, or become a complete dirty marker with `cleanShutdown:false` before a hello fails; none is rolled back. On marker or hello failure, await exact-target reconciliation of the in-flight publication, stop every launched worker and prove each job absent before guard release. Uncertain reconciliation or stop retains the guard while this process lives and fails closed. On restart, guarded residue cleanup proves `publication` and `startupMutation` jobs absent before scanning and before every selected deletion; uncertainty preserves remaining objects. Then reserve the marker, read the prior marker, reconcile any watcher orphan and complete all-role predecessor admission, allowing only the exactly owned early metadata job and verified system console host when that path was selected. A valid dirty marker follows existing recovery; incomplete publication follows the unavailable path. No mutating or core-reply worker request routes on an unknown marker postcondition.

For publication, the host's staged input and optional control files, native target and temporary sibling retain the existing crash states: before staging there is no target mutation; after staging but before native completion the owned files are residue; after native target publication but before a reply, target bytes may have changed; after a validated reply, exact target readback and cleanup still decide success. For a mutating codec call, after verified stop run the current `VerifyOnly` path over the same frozen staged source/control, without re-publication, then do the existing target readback and cleanup; an ordinary source/event inspection can retry only under its existing nonmutating semantics. If stop is unverified, target or stage is unreadable/changed, VerifyOnly fails, cleanup is incomplete, guard is lost, or the deadline has expired for caller success, block the publisher, preserve owed authority and fail closed. An exact target found after an expired call is a recovery fact, not permission to extend a settlement or provider gate.

A codec create or rewrite can occur before a core record, between core publication and journal append, or during journal/settlement reconciliation. At each point the existing `Kernel.checked` intent, outbox record, journal transaction, safety record and settlement binding remain the only durable authority; worker success does not mark them complete. Crash before the core outbox commit repeats the core's own acquisition rules; crash after commit but before journal append drains that same record; crash after append but before settlement retries the same binding; crash after core settlement but before journal acknowledgement follows the existing settlement readback and journal rewrite. A helper reply lost at any such boundary is resolved only from its own target and then the higher-level durable records, never from elapsed time or worker memory. The 120-second safety check remains armed until its current result or recovery closes it. An unreadable, stale, duplicated, foreign or unexpected-kind durable record keeps the existing unavailable, conflict or recovery branch; no warm worker infers an empty state from an absent reply.

Not-owner and consumer hand-over still use the core command and owner marker before any provider input. A D-through-H fault cancels an unbound candidate; the final provider start still runs afterward, and its `providerSafety` decides between a bound provider and a status-only client, so a fault that leaves safety authority intact does not by itself withhold the provider. A bound provider that exits, stops being current or is rebound before the check is not a refusal: the host lets its reconnect ladder settle and repeats the full check, at most three times in all, and if no ready provider returns, the client attaches status-only as after any failed reconnect, without the final-safety block. An invalidation while that reconnect settles is still a refusal. When the attempts run out with a settled ready provider, attachment takes its own cold check from that state. A status that is only unhealthy is not a refusal: failed freshness from a missing save, a stale target or pending settlement, or a journal fault that has not changed the safety generation, attaches the bound ready provider with that degraded status, its turns run disclosure-only under the runtime contract while the failure lasts, since each interaction recomputes freshness, and its timing is reported apart from the healthy targets. Join any already queued transition before closing startup workers, then allow only status-only attachment under the held guard. An uncertain provider stop, lost guard or uncertain worker closure remains fatal and cannot attach or write a clean marker. Provider-start refusal follows its existing retry or exit policy only after the candidate is settled and positively stopped; an uncertain stop prevents clean marker and attachment. A native document refusal does not trigger a second write; source or protocol mismatch invalidates that role. Worker references are invalidated on close, scope or guard loss, source drift, provider refusal and startup failure, and are not reused by later turns or restarts.
