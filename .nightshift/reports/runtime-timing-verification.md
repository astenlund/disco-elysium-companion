# Runtime timing verification

The [coordinator budget model](../features/companion-execution-runtime.md#state-acceptance-and-context-safety) requires runtime-owned phase measurements before acceptance. Core acquisition alone does not establish interaction latency or settlement headroom. The [core acquisition observation](../features/state-extraction.md#11-verification-for-the-implementation-plan-to-expand) remains separate.

The private integration checks below use the actual coordinator, core, native helpers and SessionHost with a declared fake ProviderSession. They measure runtime-owned work and delivery bookkeeping; they cannot measure model response latency or prove live guidance behavior. Complete delivery and acknowledged-binding restart passed in two recovery scenarios. The zero-new-record and one-new-record 120-second target passed on committed revision `bc04ede`; a later one-new-record measurement on `cf2337d` failed. A direct source-frozen rerun on `f2996e8` passed both cases, as recorded below. These revision-specific results do not transfer automatically to changed implementation inputs.

## Interpretation and remaining verification

Earlier development timing records under `.tmp/nightshift/backend-impl/timing-1791465843470/` contain expensive helper/store operations and one interaction that ended fresh. They do not record an interaction gate expiry. Store, core-check and drain intervals overlap, so adding their totals would overstate elapsed time. Startup `settlementPending` notices from unavailable/recovering safety are distinct from deadline expiry. The `bc04ede` measurement passed on that revision, the later `cf2337d` measurement failed its one-new-record gate, and the direct `f2996e8` rerun passed both cases on current production code.

The passing direct timing record identifies new and pending counts, retained history, payload sizes, acquisition, the shared post-publish gate, later send and acknowledgement phases, and separate private restart checks. The subsequent first-owner test ordering change did not alter production runtime or timing fixture code, so those measured runtime paths remain code-equivalent; its broader input manifest would nevertheless need a new formal Nightshift check if that gate becomes available. Helper aggregates explain cost but do not replace non-overlapping wall times. Expiry before another checked arm, expiry after arm before child start, truncated children, interrupted result or completion publication, larger backlogs and varied history sizes remain unexercised rather than being counted as zero. The 120-second gate remains fixed. These private results do not establish model latency, clean player startup, broad failure coverage or live cutover. Recovery-heavy startup and whole-turn latency remain separate player-facing follow-ups.
