# Startup delay spikes, 2026-10-14

The prior user-reported live launch reached the chat prompt in 90.738 seconds; its phase boundaries and status are recorded in [runtime timing verification](runtime-timing-verification.md#startup-stage-investigation-from-the-user-reported-live-launch). This report records sequential private spikes against the same runtime sources. No production source, real consumer marker, provider credential, or SaveGames file was changed. The probes used private repository roots or synthetic process names. Small timings are single-machine observations, not additive predictions for a player launch. The clean fixture intentionally had no save, so its safety was ready and journal healthy but freshness was failed. The recovery fixture used a private bridge release and handover; its status ended with recovering safety.

## Phase-local controls

The instrumented no-save clean control reached fake-provider binding and verified metadata-worker stop in 83.035 seconds. Its storage recovery made two `release` reservations, with two resource-inventory helpers totaling 4.517 seconds nested inside a 5.291-second phase. Its empty outbox-drain phase took 2.284 seconds, including one inventory helper taking 2.245 seconds. Startup acquisition took 12.795 seconds, including one inventory, one core publish, one core-reply validator and two runtime-file publications. Retention through its next phase marker took 6.936 seconds, with three inventory helpers totaling 6.809 seconds of nested wall. The source hashes recorded by `.tmp/startup-spikes/backend/clean-result.json` matched before and after the measured run; that manifest covers the named backend and native inventory sources, not every import.

The private recovery-heavy control reached the same fake-provider boundary in 156.601 seconds after its setup. `streamRecovery` occupied 78.172 seconds. Within that phase, 18 one-shot runtime-file publications consumed 38.790 seconds of nested helper wall, five codec calls 10.995 seconds, four resource inventories 9.199 seconds, and two core-reply validators 5.317 seconds. The other core, settlement, metadata, outbox and warm-admission work remains separately necessary. These nested helper walls overlap other measurements and must not be summed. The fixture passed with unchanged named source hashes; its local evidence is `.tmp/startup-spikes/backend/recovery-result.json`. Its release and handover setup is excluded from the 156.601-second startup measurement.

## Input and watcher boundary

The CLI currently opens readline after backend startup, provider binding and metadata-worker shutdown. A private presentation-only prototype made a draft editable at 22.898 ms process age during a simulated 120 ms startup. Enter before readiness retained an unsent draft, and readiness triggered zero host submissions; a second explicit Enter after readiness sent once. Bounded draft, quit and startup-failure cases also passed. This shows the proposed interaction state machine, not real terminal behavior or a faster answer. The governing runtime contract attaches the client after provider start and forbids automatic queuing or resending of denied input, so an early draft shell needs an agreed client design.

Two source-matched, watcher-enabled versus disabled private controls reached fake-client attachment in 82.444 and 77.217 seconds respectively. The enabled watcher start itself took 5.820 seconds and completed its identity publication before attachment. A later serial control that included real instruction assembly and FileToolAdapter preparation measured watcher start at 5.722 seconds, preparation at 0.641 seconds, fake provider callback at 0.114 ms, and durable `SessionStore.bind` at 6.620 seconds. The same private source digest was recorded before and after each paired control. The prepared overlap candidate can move only that 0.641-second preparation beside watcher start while keeping current backend serialization; full provider start awaits backend authority. Because this bound is below the requested one-second threshold, that candidate was not timed. The initial watcher fixture failed before measurement with an owned-process transport error; its 278-character scope path is a hypothesis for that failure, not a proven cause. A shorter private root was used for the passing controls, and the failed root was retained after read-only process and job-absence checks.

## Implications for a design

The low-complexity candidates are batching the two pre-marker admissions at each site, batching startup role checks with explicit per-role evidence, and sharing the already valid release reservation through storage initialization. The largest measured recovery cost is repeated launch of mutating runtime-file publication, but its warm native spike does not establish the full writer and crash contract. A startup-scoped worker extended to inventories, reply validation or codec operations would require a new agreed design for source identity, bounded requests, per-request freshness and quiescence, failure handling, deadlines and every mode's side effects; it must not silently inherit the path/hash worker's read-only authority. The existing marker inventory and other pre-worker checks cannot be credited as savings from a worker that starts later. No safety, ownership, settlement, retention or provider-send gate may be removed based on these timings. Actual startup and first-answer latency have not been remeasured with any candidate integrated.
